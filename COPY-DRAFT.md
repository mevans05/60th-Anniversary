## Squarespace page

**Headline**

> # Tom & Yvonne
> ## Sixty Years
> *Son of a six and seven.*

The subhead does the personality work without explaining itself. Family gets it
instantly; everyone else finds it charming and moves on.

**Opening**

> Our parents met at Turners Falls High School.
>
> Sixty years of marriage later, Dad still calls roughly everyone he meets "ya
> buzzard," Mom's laugh has been independently confirmed audible from five states
> away, and between them they have worn "son of a six and seven" down to the nub.
>
> They'd rather we didn't make a fuss. We're making one anyway.
>
> Join us for dinner at The Greatful Dane.

The short first line is doing the heavy lifting. "They met at Turners Falls High
School" earns the sixty years in five words, and it lets the second paragraph be
funny without having to also be sentimental — the sentiment is already banked.

**Details block**

> [DATE] · [TIME]
> The Greatful Dane, [VENUE ADDRESS]
> Buffet dinner · [DRESS CODE]
> Kindly reply by [RSVP DEADLINE]

**Optional extra line** — funny, mildly cheeky, cut it if the guest list skews formal:

> There will be a buffet and there will be wine. Whether Mom has three glasses
> is between her and the surrounding five states.

**Button**

> Let us know you're coming

**Sign-off**

> — Matt and [siblings]

## Judgment calls

Two pieces of the source material are excellent but are held back from the
public page. Both are the family's call, not mine — flagging the tradeoff, not
vetoing.

**"She's always going to be your mother, but she doesn't always have to be my
wife!"**
Genuinely funny and unmistakably him. But on the invitation it is a joke about
ending the marriage, printed on the announcement of its sixtieth year, read by a
guest list that includes people who will take it flatly. It also loses something
in print: it is a line he says *to his kids*, with timing. It belongs in the
toast, delivered live by one of you, or as the epigraph of the memory book.
That is a better home for it than a web page.

**"The Gimp"**
A family nickname with a backstory the family knows and much of the guest list
does not. Out of context and in print it reads differently than it does in the
kitchen. Recommend keeping it off the page and out of the form; it's at home in
the memory book or a toast, where whoever uses it can carry the context.

"Tommy Boy" has no such problem and could go on the page if wanted — e.g. the
sign-off could read *"— the children of Tommy Boy and Yvonne."*

## Photography note

The high-school detail unlocks the obvious hero image: **a yearbook or
high-school-era photo of the two of them, paired with a recent one.** Side by
side at the top of the page, no caption needed — the sixty years is the caption.

If a yearbook photo exists, that is the single highest-value asset for this
page, the paper invite, and the memory book. Worth a phone call to whoever has
the family albums before anything gets designed.
